Terrace / Gravity Tank Good practice

A terrace-level tank fed by a terrace pump (≥ 3.5 kg/cm² at remotest landing valve) provides gravity back-up on power/pump failure. NBC Fig. 15–17 show the arrangement; capacity is a design decision — a common allowance is shown below and is fully editable.

Fire Pump Set — Main / Standby / Jockey Table 7K/7M Note 1, Cl. 5.1.2.2

Electric main pump with a standby pump of equal capacity (diesel-engine driven, independent power) and a jockey pump to compensate minor leakage.

180 lpm is the standard value referenced throughout NBC Part F.
Notes (Table 7K/7M, Note 1 & various): "Pump" implies an electric pump plus a standby pump of equivalent capacity. Diesel-engine driven pump batteries require an independent starter battery/charger circuit. Fire pump shutoff pressure should generally not exceed 12 bar. Pumps require soft-starter / VFD starters (Cl. 5.1.2.2(m)).

Fire Brigade Collecting Head / Inlet Cl. 5.1.2.1

Street-level connections allowing the fire brigade to boost or draw from the static storage tank.

Cl. 5.1.2.1: Collecting head instantaneous male inlets — 4 nos. of 63 mm dia. (2 nos. of 63 mm dia. where pump capacity ≤ 1 400 lpm), connected to the tank via minimum 150 mm dia. GI pipe delivering at 2 250 lpm where the tank is in a basement or otherwise not approachable by fire engines. A separate draw-out collecting head (63 mm instantaneous male) connects to the tank via 100 mm dia. GI pipe with foot valve.

Yard Hydrant Layout

External yard hydrants provide fire-brigade access around the building periphery. Spacing per accepted practice (IS 3844) — default 45 m, editable per Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ) requirement.

Internal Hydrant (Landing Valve) & Hose Reel Count Cl. 5.1.1(d), (g)

One landing valve with first-aid hose reel is typically provided per fire exit staircase, per floor. Hose reel connected directly to riser/down-comer, minimum 19 mm dia (Cl. 5.1.1(d)); hydrant cabinet located within 5 m of the fire exit door (Cl. 5.1.1(g)).
