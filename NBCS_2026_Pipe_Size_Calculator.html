5 NBCS 2026 Methodology & Formulas

Step 1: Assign WSFU (Table 2, Clause 4.7.3.1)

Each fixture assigned a WSFU value based on water consumption rate, duration of use, and frequency of use. Private vs Public values differ. Parentheses = enhanced public demand.

Step 2: Calculate Total WSFU (Clause 4.7.3.2)

Sum all fixture WSFU. Service sinks excluded (Note a). Hot+cold branches use ¾ factor (Note 9). Continuous flow added to probable demand, not WSFU (Note b).

Step 3: Convert WSFU → LPM via Table 3 (Probable Simultaneous Demand)

Use Hunter's probability curve (Table 3 / Fig. 2). Flush tank column for tank systems, flush valve column for valve systems. Log-log interpolation between table entries. For FU > 1000, both columns converge (Note 4).

LPM = interpolated value from NBCS 2026 Table 3 (exact data points, log-log interpolation)

Step 4: Available Head for Friction

H_avail = (P_source − P_residual) × 10.197 − H_elevation [m of water]

1 bar = 10.197 m H₂O. NBCS 4.7.5: Max pressure = 0.42 N/mm² (4.2 bar); pressure reducing valves required above this.

Step 5: Friction Head Loss — Hazen-Williams (Clause 4.7.4)

hf = 10.67 × L_eq × Q^1.852 / (C^1.852 × d^4.87)

L_eq = L × (1 + fitting %). Q in m³/s, d in m, C = Hazen-Williams coefficient. Nomogram in Annex C.

Step 6: Velocity

V = Q / (π × d² / 4)

Acceptable range: 0.6–3.0 m/s (per NBCS 4.7.5 adequate pressure requirements).

Step 7: Select Pipe Size

Choose smallest DN satisfying: (a) velocity ≤ limit, AND (b) friction head loss ≤ available head. Verify residual pressure ≥ minimum required.

Pressure Conversions

ΔP (bar) = hf × 0.0981 | ΔP (kPa) = hf × 9.81 | 1 N/mm² = 1 MPa = 10 bar
